## Core idea

> Gold fell about 4.5% in a week when US inflation came in hot, because the market traded the Fed's likely response, not the inflation number itself.

**Content type:** Macro explainer
**Takeaway for the reader:** Ask what a data print does to interest rates before asking what it does to gold.

## Posts

**Post 1 (stands alone):**

> US inflation came in hotter than expected this week. Gold fell about 4.5%, its worst week since June.
>
> If gold is an inflation hedge, that looks backwards. It isn't. The market wasn't trading inflation. It was trading what the Fed does about it.

**Post 2:**

> The chain went like this.
>
> Core CPI printed 0.3% against 0.2% expected.
> Markets moved to price a rate hike in December.
> The 2-year yield rose about 35 bps. The 10-year went above 5%.
> The dollar reached its highest since April 2025.
>
> Gold pays no interest. When cash and bonds pay more, holding gold costs more.

**Post 3:**

> Worth noticing: US stocks had their best week since August. So this wasn't panic selling across the board. It was a rates and dollar story, and gold was on the wrong side of both.

**Post 4:**

> Next week: US retail sales on Tuesday, Fed minutes on Wednesday.
>
> On our read, gold stays under pressure while it holds below roughly $3,900. That view is wrong if price reclaims the $3,903–3,934 area. Thursday's low near $3,852 is the level below.
>
> The habit to keep: before asking what a number means for gold, ask what it means for rates.
